When monitoring live engine data via an OBD2 scanner app, one of the most critical screens is the **oxygen (O2) sensor live graph**. Knowing how to interpret these voltage waveforms and Air-Fuel Ratio (AFR) metrics allows you to diagnose everything from vacuum leaks and lazy injectors to failing catalytic converters before a Check Engine light appears.

## 1. Wideband Air-Fuel Ratio vs. Narrowband O2 Sensors

Modern engine layouts utilize distinct sensor types upstream and downstream of the catalytic converter:

* **Wideband A/F Sensor (Upstream - Bank 1, Sensor 1):** Positioned before the catalyst, it measures exact air-fuel ratios linearly across a broad spectrum, typically displayed as a Lambda ($\lambda$) value where $1.0$ represents stoichiometric balance (14.7:1 for gasoline).
* **Narrowband O2 Sensor (Downstream - Sensor 2):** Positioned after the catalyst, it acts strictly as a compliance monitor. Its voltage swings dynamically between **0.1V (lean condition)** and **0.9V (rich condition)**.

## 2. Reading Diagnostic Waveforms on Your Scanner

1. **Flatlined Upstream Sensor:** If the upstream sensor voltage freezes or fails to switch under throttle input, the internal heater circuit is likely dead, forcing the ECU into open-loop fueling.
2. **Downstream Mirroring Upstream Waveforms:** If Sensor 2 mimics the rapid oscillations of Sensor 1, **the catalytic converter has failed** and lost its oxygen storage capacity, triggering a classic `P0420` efficiency fault.
3. **Fuel Trim Correlation:** If Short Term Fuel Trims (STFT) continuously pull or add more than 10% fuel to correct the mixture, investigate unmetered intake air leaks or leaking fuel injectors.

## Conclusion

Mastering live O2 sensor waveform analysis via the OBD2 bus enables technicians to pinpoint combustion anomalies quickly without guesswork.
