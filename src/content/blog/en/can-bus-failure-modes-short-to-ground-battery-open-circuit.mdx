The **Controller Area Network (CAN Bus)** functions as the central nervous system of a modern vehicle. When physical damage occurs within the twisted-pair wiring harness (CAN High and CAN Low), the entire communication grid can fail, disabling multiple control modules simultaneously.

## 1. The Three Primary CAN Bus Electrical Failure Modes

### 1. Open Circuit
Happens when one wire in the twisted pair breaks (often inside door boot grommets due to repetitive flexing).
* **Effect:** The differential signaling breaks down and terminal resistance drops to $120\ \Omega$, causing intermittent communication faults (`U`-codes) as vibration separates the broken strands.

### 2. Short to Ground
Occurs when **CAN Low** or **CAN High** touches bare metal chassis ground (0V).
* **Effect:** Differential voltage collapse halts message transmission across all connected transceivers, shutting down network communication.

### 3. Short to Battery (+12V)
The most destructive fault, happening when harness insulation melts against a constant +12V power feed.
* **Effect:** High voltage instantly destroys transceiver IC chips (such as TJA1050 or MCP2551) across connected control modules.

## 2. Diagnostic Testing via Multimeter and Scope

* **Static Resistance (Battery Disconnected):**
  * Healthy network: **60 $\Omega$**.
  * Reading **120 $\Omega$**: Open circuit on one termination resistor branch.
  * Reading **0 $\Omega$ or infinity ($\infty$)**: Direct short or total severed wire.

## Conclusion

Understanding CAN Bus electrical failure modes accelerates troubleshooting harness damage in complex collision and wiring repairs.
